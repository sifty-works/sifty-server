# Assessment

What the user gets from assessment: every vacancy in their lists carries one number, Fits you, that says how well the vacancy fits their goals, and a counter of its requirements that says how well they fit the vacancy. Both are computed by code from the user's own data and never hide a vacancy; after a correction the open vacancy changes at once and the others follow in the background. Vacancies are read by code; a model is used only for one check of a shortlisted vacancy.

## What the user states

**Goals, per profile:** target roles; minimum compensation with currency, period and tax basis; engagement, working time, work modes and locations, each as four lists (see Goal lists below); the weekly hours of part-time; willingness to relocate, which Fits you does not read and the Shortlist check does; time zones, chosen from six fixed UTC bands; other requirements, read by the Shortlist check and not by Fits you. An empty compensation field, weekly hours, or a criterion whose four lists are all empty, of a profile other than the first, is taken from the first profile when assessing (see `profiles.md`).

**Goal lists.** Work mode (Remote, Hybrid, Onsite), engagement (Contract, Employment, Internship), working time (Full-time, Part-time) and locations (countries, regions and cities, as typed places) each have four lists: Preferred, Possible, Unwanted and Never. An option is in at most one list, and an option in none is neutral. A vacancy's value is looked up in the lists of its criterion; when it states several values ("Remote or Onsite", several places) the best list counts, and for one place the stricter of the entries that cover it holds (Preferred EU with Never Germany makes Berlin a Never). A place matches an entry by country, region or city; an entry with a city in Unwanted or Never matches only a vacancy that names that city, while one in Preferred or Possible also matches a vacancy that names the country alone.

**My data, shared by all profiles:** skills with a level; skills the user does not have; "Confirmed by you", a list of "You have: …" and "You do not have: …" statements and of free-text facts about the user; experience, from which code derives total years, years in leadership roles, years in engineering roles, founder experience and work in the last year; languages; current location; work authorizations by country or by region, each eligible or needing a visa, where a region covers its countries (the EU covers its member states).

## Fits you

Fits you is a number from 0 to 100 and the only number the user sees. Each criterion below is compared on its own and gives a match, a mismatch or "cannot be compared", with a reason in words and the source of the fact it rests on. The number is the weighted average of the criteria, rounded. A criterion that cannot be compared enters it as half-way (0.5) at half its weight, so a vacancy that states little is pulled towards the middle and does not reach 100 on one or two matching criteria; a criterion with weight 0 counts for nothing. Because the number is recomputed with a new scoring version, every result is recomputed once after an update. The result says on how many criteria it was compared ("scored on 3 of 6") and lists the reasons, among them those of the criteria that could not be compared. A criterion the profile states no goal for is not counted. A profile with no goals gives no number: goals are target roles, a minimum compensation, an engagement, working time, work mode or locations list with an option in it, and time zones; work authorizations and languages alone are not goals. A vacancy whose full description is unavailable gives no number, and neither does one of which nothing can be compared with the goals ("Nothing the vacancy states can be compared with your goals"): criteria that cannot be compared never make a number by themselves. A value that is in none of the profile's lists has no effect: its criterion is left out of the number and of "scored on", and its reason says so ("Hybrid — not in your lists").

| Criterion | Starting weight | Strictness | How it is compared |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role | 0.25 | prefer | best word overlap of the vacancy title with a target role; a match from half of the words, "Partly matches your target roles" below that, and "far from your target roles" only when no word matches |
| Money | 0.20 | prefer | `min(1, top of the vacancy range / user's minimum)` after conversion |
| Location | 0.075 | prefer | the vacancy's places against the locations lists; see Location below |
| Remote restriction (work authorization) | 0.075 | must | see Location below |
| Time zone | 0.10 | prefer | 1 when the vacancy and the user share a band |
| Work mode | 0.04 | prefer | the vacancy's work modes against the work mode lists, see below; Never is a broken must |
| Engagement | 0.03 | prefer | the vacancy's engagement against the engagement lists; a vacancy that states no engagement cannot be compared; Never is a broken must |
| Hours | 0.03 | prefer | full-time or part-time against the working time lists, and for a part-time vacancy in Preferred or Possible the weekly hours when the user states them; ranges that do not overlap are a mismatch; Never is a broken must |
| Languages | 0 | must | every required language of the vacancy is among the languages in My data; without a language in My data it cannot be compared |
| Requirements | 0.15 | prefer | the requirements score below |

Weights live in configuration; the strictness of each criterion is fixed in the code for now.

**Lists.** For a criterion with lists, Preferred is a match of utility 1, Possible a match of 0.75, Unwanted a mismatch of utility 0.25 and Never a broken must (see Must and prefer). A value in none of the lists leaves the criterion out of the number ("scored on 3 of 6" drops by one) and says so ("Hybrid — not in your lists"); a vacancy that states no value cannot be compared ("Work mode not stated"). A criterion whose four lists are empty is not counted. Reasons name the list: "Remote — preferred", "Hybrid — possible", "United States — unwanted", "Onsite — in your Never list". When the profile wants remote work (Remote in Preferred or Possible) and the vacancy's remote work is restricted or its places are unknown, Work mode is left to Remote restriction and cannot be compared ("Remote — see Work authorization"). Unrestricted remote work counts as Preferred in Location when the user has a Preferred place, and is neutral otherwise. Willingness to relocate changes nothing here: say Onsite is Possible or Unwanted instead.

**Must and prefer.** A mismatch on a prefer criterion only lowers the number. A mismatch on a must criterion (languages, remote restriction), and a value in a Never list, is a broken must: the number is at most 15 when the fact comes from the vacancy's own text or the employer's field, and the vacancy is listed after every vacancy without a broken must in Best fit. When the fact is only an aggregator's field, the number is at most 40 and the position in Best fit does not change. A must that cannot be compared costs nothing and gives a reason ("Work authorization not stated"). The reasons of broken musts come first, then the other mismatches, then the musts that could not be compared; they read like "Onsite — in your Never list" or "Requires Mandarin — not in your languages".

**Money.** The vacancy's range is converted to the user's currency by rates from configuration. A sum without a tax note is gross. A sum without a period is yearly; a monthly sum is multiplied by 12, a daily sum by 260, an hourly sum by 2080. Money cannot be compared when the vacancy states no top of its range or no currency, when a currency has no rate, or when the vacancy's tax basis differs from the user's.

**Location.** The Location criterion compares the vacancy's places with the profile's locations lists; with all four empty it is not counted. A place is in a list when it lies in an entry's country or region, and in the entry's city when both name a city; the best list of the vacancy's places counts, and a place in no list is left out. A remote vacancy whose places are worldwide ones ("Anywhere", "Worldwide") and that states no time zone is unrestricted remote: it matches Remote restriction, and Location as described under Lists. A remote vacancy that names no place, or whose only restriction is a time zone, cannot be compared on either, and the Location tile shows ?. Restricted remote ("US only", "EU") is checked against the user's work authorizations, not against where the user lives: covered when an eligible authorization covers one of its countries, not covered when the user states authorizations for its countries and none is eligible, unknown when the user states none or a place is unknown. Remote restriction cannot be compared for a vacancy that is not remote. When remote work is restricted or its places are unknown and the user wants remote work, Work mode is left to Remote restriction and cannot be compared. Onsite and hybrid vacancies are not checked against work authorizations. Only an authorization marked eligible covers a country. Composite labels ("Berlin, Germany", "Remote (US or Canada)") are read with a vocabulary of countries and regions; a part the vocabulary does not know is taken as a city, and a place that names no country or region is unknown.

**Time zone.** The vacancy's bands come from a stated time zone (an offset such as UTC+2 or an abbreviation such as CET, widened by "± N hours"), then from time-zone phrases in the text, then from the countries and regions of its locations.

**Buckets.** Strong is 75 and above, Partial is 55 to 74, Weak is below 55, and Not scored has no number. The Fit filter of the Inbox uses these buckets.

## Requirements

- A vacancy's requirements are the lines of its requirement sections found by code (in a text with no such section, the lines that state a requirement), each with its level, required or nice to have, from its section and marker words.
- **Skills.** Every skill the skills vocabulary finds in those lines is one skill of the vacancy: its name, Required or Nice to have (Required when it is in both), the level the vacancy demands and the line it came from. A skill named in several lines is one skill with the highest demand. A line that names alternatives ("C, C++ or Rust", "one of Go or Java") gives one skill naming them, met by the best of them.
- **Demanded level.** The skills vocabulary holds a table that reads a level from the line: "1+" years and the words familiarity, exposure and basic mean Beginner; "2+" and "3+" years and the words solid, strong, proficient and hands-on mean Proficient; "4+" years and more and the words deep, expert and extensive mean Expert; a line with no years and no word of the table demands Any, that is, having the skill is enough. Years and words beyond the table follow the nearest rule; of several, the highest counts, and a range of years counts by its lower end. Years apply to every skill of their line.
- **Verdict of a skill.** The user's level comes from Skills in My data (Beginner, Proficient, Expert) or from the skills the user does not have (No). A level at or above the demand is ✓; one level below is borderline, ≈; two below, or No, is ✕; a skill that is in neither list of My data is ?. Any is met by any level.
- **Other requirements.** A line that names no known skill stays a requirement row, decided by code against My data: ✓ or ✕ when the requirement is confirmed by the user as had or as not had, and ? otherwise. A requirement that asks for years of experience is not decided by code; the derived years are shown as evidence. A line that names a skill is not a row: its skills carry it.
- The counter "✓ 4 · ≈ 1 · ✕ 1 · ? 6" counts skills and rows together; "≈" is shown only when there is a borderline one. It is shown in the Inbox row and on the vacancy page.
- The requirements score is the average over skills and rows of ✓ = 1, ≈ = 0.5 and ✕ = 0, with required ones counted twice and nice-to-have ones once; undecided ones are left out. Without a decided one it cannot be compared. It enters Fits you as the Requirements criterion, and a requirement decided ✕ is a mismatch of it.
- Model matching of requirements is not part of the first version. A later checker can be connected through the same interface, and only after it decides hand-labelled examples better than code.

## Facts of a vacancy

Facts are found by code, without a model, for every vacancy visible in a profile, as the background work "Analyzing vacancy" (see `application.md`): money, work mode, engagement, place, time zone, hours, required languages, the requirement lines, the offer, and eight key facts: the years of experience asked, the seniority, the company stage, the deadline for applications, the length of a contract, a citizenship or security-clearance requirement, relocation support and visa sponsorship. Structured fields the source states, the location, the tags and labelled lines ("Salary: …", "Location: …", "Time zone: …") are read first, then vocabularies over the whole text; each fact keeps the excerpt it was found in. When the address is an aggregator's, a value stated in the vacancy text replaces a field's value of the same kind; when it is the employer's own page, a field or labelled line replaces a vocabulary's value. Fits you, the requirements counter, the tiles and the offer read these facts and nothing else.

A fact says only what the vacancy says; when the vacancy does not say it, the fact is absent and the tile shows "?", never a guess with ✓.

- **Years, seniority, company stage.** Years is the most years the requirement lines ask for experience in general or in the role's field ("5+ years of backend experience" is 5, "five years" and "5-year" count too; a range such as "3-5 years" counts its lower end), never the years of a company's history or product, and not a line marked nice to have. When no requirement line asks years, a sentence anywhere outside the company, benefits, equal-opportunity and how-to-apply parts that asks "N years of/in/with … experience" is read; "founded 12 years ago" and "for over 10 years we have …" are never read. Seniority (Intern, Junior, Mid, Senior, Staff, Principal, Lead, Head, Director, VP, C-level) comes from the title first, then from a plain statement in the text ("this is a senior-level role"); a look-alike such as "Lead Generation" or "Chief of Staff" gives none, and a title that names several levels gives the highest. Company stage comes only from the opening text and the company sections, never from a description of investors or customers ("our customers are VC-backed startups"): Public, Series A to E (the latest named), Seed, Bootstrapped, Profitable, Growth (only an explicit "growth-stage" or "scale-up"; revenue growth gives none), a raise without a series ("Raised $120M"), Early stage ("early-stage", "pre-seed") and VC-backed ("backed by … investors", "venture-backed"), the first of these in that order that the text states. A raise states its amount when the same sentence does, and its valuation after it ("Series D, $1.37B, valued at $7.87B"); an amount that is revenue, ARR or users is not a raise. The phrases are in the vocabulary shipped with the application.
- **Engagement.** Contract comes only from a statement of the engagement ("contract role", "contractor basis", "B2B contract", "1099", "fixed-term", "freelance", "as a contractor"), not from "smart contract", "contract management", "contractors" in equal-opportunity text, a negated statement ("this is not a contract role", "do not apply to fixed term roles") "temporary work visa" and "permanent residency" (a word about work authorization), or the company being a government or federal contractor; a line of equal-opportunity or self-identification boilerplate gives none. A label such as Type, Job type or Employment type followed by Contract, Freelance or B2B (also as a table cell under such a header), "N-month contract" and "Contract, N+ months" give contract. An internship also comes from an intern in the title, "hiring interns" and "Interns are paid", not from "mentor interns". An internship is its own value, Internship, never contract. "Full-time" and "part-time" are hours and never make Employment; Employment comes from "permanent", "W-2", "full-time employee", "employment contract", "employee position", or a site field that says permanent or employee. Text under requirements, nice-to-have and how-to-apply gives no engagement.
- **Place.** A restriction anywhere in the text, including benefits, eligibility notes and application questions, gives the place ("Remote, US-based", "Remote - Poland", "anywhere in Canada", "must reside in Spain", "authorized to work in X", "must be a US citizen") and replaces a site field's place, for an employer's vacancy too ("West Coast (U.S.) based" is the United States). "Anywhere" or "Remote" as the place in an aggregator's field, with nothing in the text, is no place, but "Remote" and TELECOMMUTE in a location field are the work mode Remote, and the countries listed with them stay. A restriction in the title ("Remote - LATAM") beats a softer phrase in the text, and a place that describes customers, users or developers served is no restriction. "Work from anywhere" and "worldwide remote" are explicit worldwide statements. A soft request to overlap some working hours with a team's time zone is not a time-zone restriction. A worldwide statement in the text gives Worldwide only when no site field names a place; a country or city in a site field stays, while a restriction in the text still replaces it. Worldwide comes only from an explicit statement ("work from anywhere in the world", "hiring worldwide", "global and remote-based") and never beside a country, region or time-zone restriction, whether it comes from the text or from a site field. A value that names no place ("LOCATION", "na", a two-letter word that is not a country code) is dropped; a country code from a site field shows as the country name ("PL" as Poland).
- **Money.** Pay is only the role's pay: amounts tied to bonuses, stipends, budgets, allowances, learning, equipment, referrals, sign-on or per-task payments, a range written backwards, and an amount below a plausible floor for its period (and any amount with no period below 10,000) are not money. "$175K–$225K + equity", "£115k + equity", "272 000 zł – 368 000 zł" and a typo such as "$180,000-$200,00" are read.
- **Work mode.** "In-person N days a week", "Willing to work from <city>", "Location: In person" and "in-person" for the role give Onsite; a share of the time in the office ("at least 40%") and days in the office with remote days give Hybrid; "<city> or Remote" gives Remote and Onsite. In-person interviews, events and meetings give no mode, "in-office" in a perk such as a stipend gives none, a business "hybrid model" with no word about the workplace gives none, and "hybrid" with freedom to work remotely stays Hybrid. A header segment such as "NYC or REMOTE (US, Europe)" gives Remote and Onsite. "This role is based in our <city> HQ" gives Onsite, "50% telecommuting" gives Hybrid, and a location field such as "In person, San Francisco Bay Area" gives Onsite and the place.

## In the lists

- Every row in all four lists shows Fits you as a filled mark with its bucket, and the requirements counter. The tooltip reads "Fits you 82: Strong", followed by the counter when requirements are read (" · ✓ 4 · ≈ 1 · ✕ 1 · ? 6").
- Empty states of Fits you: "Not compared yet", "Full job description is unavailable", "Your profile states no goals to compare", "Nothing the vacancy states can be compared with your goals".
- Empty states of the counter: "Analysing requirements…", "Not analysed yet", "No requirements stated", "Not checked yet" (no requirement is decided), "Full job description is unavailable".
- A result is out of date when the profile's goals, the work authorizations, the languages, the weights, the rates or the scoring version changed, or the vacancy's text changed. It is dimmed, still showing its old number, until the new one is ready, and the Fit filter counts it as Not scored. A change of the vacancy's title, location, tags or stated fields without a change of its text is not dimmed; its result is recomputed after the collection run that brought the change.
- **Best fit** sorts by Fits you, highest first, then by id; dimmed results and vacancies without a number come after the current ones, by id; vacancies with a broken must come last, by their number. It is the default sort of Inbox and Shortlist.
- **Newest** sorts by publication date, newest first; vacancies with no publication date come last, by when they were first seen, then by id.
- The chosen sort is remembered in the browser (see `inbox.md`).

## On the vacancy page

From top to bottom:

1. **Fits you band.** "Fits you" above one large number coloured by its bucket (Strong, Partial, Weak; no colour for Not scored), the reason in place of the number when the vacancy is not scored, and the "Refresh source" button (see `collection.md`); the reasons and "scored on" are not shown there, since the tiles and What they ask say the same. When any tile's value is in a Never list, a "Never" line above the band names each one ("Work mode: Onsite is in your Never list"). On the right, the key facts of the vacancy, each with its icon and name, a verdict mark ✓ (green), ✕ (red) or ? (grey), the vacancy's value or "Not stated", and a line comparing the value with the user's goal. Four are cards, in the order Money, Location, Work mode, Contract; the rest are rows of the Key facts table below them, in the order Time zone, Languages, Hours, Work authorization, with mismatches first, then unknown, then matches, followed by Experience, Seniority and Company stage, and then, each only when the vacancy states it, Apply by, Contract length, Citizenship or clearance, Relocation and Visa. These five show ? with no goal and do not change Fits you. Apply by shows the date and how many days are left ("12 days left", "Closes today") and is ✕ once the date has passed ("Closed 3 days ago"); a date without a year is read as the one nearest to the day the vacancy was first seen. Contract length shows the length with its unit ("6 months", "16 weeks", "4–8 months"), Citizenship or clearance the requirement as the text names it ("US citizen", "Active Secret clearance", "U.S. person (ITAR)", "Public trust"), Relocation "Offered" or "Not offered" and Visa "Sponsorship offered" or "No sponsorship". A card also shows the conversion when there is one and the line.

   |---|---|
   | Money | Money |
   | Location | Location |
   | Work mode | Work mode |
   | Contract | Engagement |
   | Time zone | Time zone |

   One criterion, one tile, so a tile shows ✕ when its criterion is a mismatch, ✓ when it matches and ? otherwise, and never disagrees with the number; its line is the reason of its criterion ("Onsite — in your Never list"). Each tile says whether its criterion was compared, which is whether the profile has a goal for it: a minimum compensation, a locations, work mode, engagement or working time list, or time zones. Languages and Work authorization have no goal of their own and count when the vacancy requires a language, or is remote. In the Key facts table a row whose value is not stated shows "Not stated" with ?, and only when the profile has a goal for it; without a goal it is not shown. Time zone shows the stated time zone, or else the bands of the vacancy's countries; Languages the required languages; Hours full-time or part-time with the weekly hours; Work authorization the place a remote vacancy is restricted to, or "No restriction". Money shows the vacancy's own sum compactly: the currency symbol where one exists ($, €, £) and otherwise the code ("CHF 120k"), then the amount or the range, written in thousands or millions with one decimal when it is not whole ("$190–225k", "€7.5k", "€900–1.2M") and in full below a thousand, then the period the vacancy states ("a year"). Its conversion, "≈ €207,000", stands beside that sum and is absent when no rate is known. A tile also carries the list its value fell in (`bucket`: preferred, possible, unwanted or never; absent when the value is in no list or the criterion has none), so the client can colour it. The Contract tile shows "Not stated" when the vacancy states no engagement and no longer includes hours. The Location tile shows its places in the list row's short form (see `inbox.md`), with the full text on hover when shortened.

   Experience, Seniority and Company stage have no criterion behind them and no goal: each exists only when the vacancy states the value, and none of them changes Fits you. Experience shows the years asked as "5+ years" with the derived total years of My data as its line ("You have 9 years"): ✓ when the user's years reach the years asked, ✕ when they fall short by more than two years, and ? otherwise, also when My data has no experience. Seniority and Company stage show the value ("Senior", "Series B, $40M") with ?.

2. **What they ask.** "N requirements" with the counter, then the groups Required and Nice to have. Each group shows its skills, each with the level the vacancy demands, the user's level from My data and its verdict, then its requirement rows, each with its verdict and on the right the evidence from My data ("8 years").
   - A skill carries a level control with Expert, Proficient, Beginner and No. Choosing a level writes My data: the skill at that level in Skills, taking it out of the skills the user does not have; No puts it among the skills the user does not have and takes it out of Skills. A skill the user holds under another name of the vocabulary changes that entry and is not added twice. Clearing an answer is done in the profile.
   - A requirement row has two controls, **+** ("I have this") and **−** ("I do not have this"), which add the line to Confirmed by you as "You have: …" or "You do not have: …".
   - After an answer this vacancy is recomputed at once, on the page and in its Inbox row; other vacancies follow in the background. A notice names the change, in one sentence:

     - a statement for Confirmed by you: "Added "Driving licence" to Confirmed by you as something you have (change in your profile)." or "… as something you do not have (change in your profile).";

3. **What they offer** and **Listing text**, described in `inbox.md`.
4. **Shortlist check**, in Shortlist and Archive when a check exists (see below).

## Shortlist check

- When a vacancy enters Shortlist, one model call compares the user's data with the vacancy's requirements. When that call decides no requirement at all, it is made once more, and the second answer is the answer. The model receives the vacancy's facts and text, the accepted data of My data and the profile including other requirements, and the text of documents marked "Use as evidence"; never contacts, legal name or application form answers (see `profiles.md`).
- Its answer is shown next to the requirements that code left as ?, each with a short reason and the excerpt it relies on; a skill that is ? goes to it as the line it came from, and its answer is shown beside that skill. It changes neither Fits you nor the counter.
- When any of its inputs changes, the check is marked "Based on your data of <date>" and runs again the next time the vacancy page is opened. A change of profile or My data therefore makes no model call by itself.
- States: "Checking…", "Waiting for the model: N ahead", and "Check failed: <reason>" with a "Retry" button shown only after a failure or an interruption. There is no Assess or Reassess button.
- The check uses the model chosen for it in Settings; by default it must be a local model or one the user chose explicitly.

## When things are recomputed

| Change | What is recomputed | Model calls |
|---|---|---|
| New or changed vacancy text | its facts and requirements are found; its Fits you for every profile, after that | none |
| A vacancy becomes visible in a profile without having been analysed | its facts and requirements are found | none |
| A change of goals or My data that enters Fits you | Fits you of every vacancy for the affected profiles, in the background; Shortlist checks marked outdated | none |
| A level on a skill, or + or − on a requirement row | this vacancy at once, the rest in the background | none |
| A change of weights or vocabularies | everything | none |
| A new version of the application that reads texts differently | the facts of every vacancy are found again, in the background | none |
| A vacancy enters Shortlist | the Shortlist check | 1, or 2 when the first decides nothing |
| A shortlisted vacancy with an outdated check is opened | the Shortlist check | 1, or 2 when the first decides nothing |

Fits you is recomputed in the background by code, with no model call: at start, after a collection run, and after My data or a profile is saved or a profile is created. Only results that are missing or out of date are recomputed, and the work repeats until none is left, so a change made while it runs is applied too.

Vacancy texts are analysed in the background, one at a time, in the order the vacancies arrived, and only for vacancies visible in at least one profile; Fits you is recomputed for them after their analysis, not before. Analysis is code and makes no model call.

## Not carried over

- A separate You fit number; the requirements counter and the Requirements criterion replace it.
- The detailed model assessment: its summary, evidence, unknown employer details, "Confirmed mismatches", "Candidate facts / questions", provenance, provider choice, queue status line, the warnings about an older snapshot and about scores being updated, the Assess and Assess now buttons, and the queue switch in Settings. The Shortlist check replaces it.
- The model matching pass over all vacancies.
- The lists Matches, Mismatches, Missing and To clarify, which were computed and stored but no longer shown.
- The separate "Candidate facts and corrections" form with its scope; Confirmed by you holds these facts.
- A world city table for time zones; countries and regions are enough.
- Fits you shown twice on the page, and the note pointing to a tab where the assessment no longer is.
- Freshness flags of the profile and the "Refresh scoring" button.
- The background order following the sort switch.
- The "AI assessment" filter of the Inbox.
