# Assessment

What the user gets from assessment: every vacancy in their lists carries one number, Fit, that says how well the vacancy fits their goals, and the vacancy page says which of its requirements the user meets. Both are computed by code from the user's own data and never hide a vacancy; after a correction the open vacancy changes at once and the others follow in the background. Vacancies are read by code; a model is used only for one check of a shortlisted vacancy.

## What the user states

**Goals, per profile:** target roles; minimum compensation with currency, period and tax basis; engagement, working time, work modes, locations and the company's domain, each as four lists (see Goal lists below); the weekly hours of part-time; willingness to relocate, which Fit does not read and the Shortlist check does; time zones, chosen from six fixed UTC bands; other requirements, read by the Shortlist check and not by Fit. An empty compensation field, weekly hours, or a criterion whose four lists are all empty, of a profile other than the first, is taken from the first profile when assessing (see `profiles.md`).

**Goal lists.** Work mode (Remote, Hybrid, Onsite), engagement (Contract, Employment, Internship), working time (Full-time, Part-time) locations (countries, regions and cities, as typed places) and domain (the 22 domains listed under Facts) each have four lists: Preferred, Possible, Unwanted and Never. An option is in at most one list, and an option in none is neutral. A vacancy's value is looked up in the lists of its criterion; when it states several values ("Remote or Onsite", several places) the best list counts, and for one place the stricter of the entries that cover it holds (Preferred EU with Never Germany makes Berlin a Never). A place matches an entry by country, region or city; an entry with a city in Unwanted or Never matches only a vacancy that names that city, while one in Preferred or Possible also matches a vacancy that names the country alone.

**My data, shared by all profiles:** skills with a level; skills the user does not have; "Confirmed by you", a list of "You have: …" and "You do not have: …" statements and of free-text facts about the user; experience, from which code derives total years, years in leadership roles, years in engineering roles, founder experience and work in the last year; languages; current location; work authorizations by country or by region, each eligible or needing a visa, where a region covers its countries (the EU covers its member states).

## Fit

Fit is a number from 0 to 100 and the only number the user sees. Each criterion below is compared on its own and gives a match, a mismatch or "cannot be compared", with a reason in words and the source of the fact it rests on. The number is the weighted average of the criteria, rounded. A criterion that cannot be compared enters it as half-way (0.5) at half its weight, so a vacancy that states little is pulled towards the middle and does not reach 100 on one or two matching criteria; a criterion with weight 0 counts for nothing. The scoring version rises when the comparison changes, as it did when Work mode became compared by its lists always and an authorization list that names none of a restricted vacancy's countries became a mismatch, so every result is recomputed once after an update. The result says on how many criteria it was compared ("scored on 3 of 6") and lists the reasons, among them those of the criteria that could not be compared. A criterion the profile states no goal for is not counted. A profile with no goals gives no number: goals are target roles, a minimum compensation, an engagement, working time, work mode, locations or domain list with an option in it, and time zones; work authorizations and languages alone are not goals. A vacancy whose full description is unavailable gives no number, and neither does one of which nothing can be compared with the goals ("Nothing the vacancy states can be compared with your goals"): criteria that cannot be compared never make a number by themselves. A value that is in none of the profile's lists has no effect: its criterion is left out of the number and of "scored on", and its reason says so ("Hybrid — not in your lists").

| Criterion | Starting weight | Strictness | How it is compared |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role | 0.25 | prefer | best word overlap of the vacancy title with a target role; a match from half of the words, "Partly matches your target roles" below that, and "far from your target roles" only when no word matches |
| Money | 0.20 | prefer | `min(1, top of the vacancy range / user's minimum)` after conversion |
| Location | 0.075 | prefer | the vacancy's places against the locations lists; see Location below |
| Remote restriction (work authorization) | 0.075 | must | see Location below |
| Time zone | 0.10 | prefer | 1 when the vacancy and the user share a band |
| Work mode | 0.04 | prefer | the vacancy's work modes against the work mode lists, see below; Never is a broken must |
| Engagement | 0.03 | prefer | the vacancy's engagement against the engagement lists; a vacancy that states no engagement cannot be compared; Never is a broken must |
| Domain | 0.10 | prefer | the company's main domain, read by the model, against the domain lists; a vacancy without a domain cannot be compared ("Domain not stated"); Never caps the number at 40, since the model's reading can be wrong |
| Hours | 0.03 | prefer | full-time or part-time against the working time lists, and for a part-time vacancy in Preferred or Possible the weekly hours when the user states them; ranges that do not overlap are a mismatch; Never is a broken must |
| Languages | 0 | must | every required language of the vacancy is among the languages in My data; without a language in My data it cannot be compared |
| Requirements | 0.15 | prefer | the requirements score below |

Weights live in configuration; the strictness of each criterion is fixed in the code for now.

**Lists.** For a criterion with lists, Preferred is a match of utility 1, Possible a match of 0.75, Unwanted a mismatch of utility 0.25 and Never a broken must (see Must and prefer). A value in none of the lists leaves the criterion out of the number ("scored on 3 of 6" drops by one) and says so ("Hybrid — not in your lists"); a vacancy that states no value cannot be compared ("Work mode not stated", "Domain not stated"; a company the model has not read yet is not penalised). A criterion whose four lists are empty is not counted. Reasons name the list: "Remote — preferred", "Hybrid — possible", "United States — unwanted", "Onsite — in your Never list", "Exchange — preferred", "Gambling — in your Never list". Work mode is always compared by its lists, whatever the remote work is restricted to; where the user may work is the question of Work authorization. Unrestricted remote work counts as Preferred in Location when the user has a Preferred place, and is neutral otherwise. Willingness to relocate changes nothing here: say Onsite is Possible or Unwanted instead.

**Must and prefer.** A mismatch on a prefer criterion only lowers the number. A mismatch on a must criterion (languages, remote restriction), and a value in a Never list, is a broken must: the number is at most 15 when the fact comes from the vacancy's own text or the employer's field, and the vacancy is listed after every vacancy without a broken must in Best fit. When the fact is only an aggregator's field, or is read by the model (the company's domain), the number is at most 40 and the position in Best fit does not change. A must that cannot be compared costs nothing and gives a reason ("Work authorization not stated"). The reasons of broken musts come first, then the other mismatches, then the musts that could not be compared; they read like "Onsite — in your Never list" or "Requires Mandarin — not in your languages".

**Money.** The vacancy's range is converted to the user's currency by rates from configuration. A sum without a tax note is gross. A sum without a period is yearly; a monthly sum is multiplied by 12, a daily sum by 260, an hourly sum by 2080. Money cannot be compared when the vacancy states no top of its range or no currency, when a currency has no rate, or when the vacancy's tax basis differs from the user's.

**Location.** The Location criterion compares the vacancy's places with the profile's locations lists; with all four empty it is not counted. A place is in a list when it lies in an entry's country or region, and in the entry's city when both name a city; the best list of the vacancy's places counts, and a place in no list is left out. A remote vacancy whose places are worldwide ones ("Anywhere", "Worldwide") and that states no time zone is unrestricted remote: it matches Remote restriction, and Location as described under Lists. A remote vacancy that names no place, or whose only restriction is a time zone, cannot be compared on either, and the Location tile shows ?. Restricted remote ("US only", "EU") is checked against the user's work authorizations, not against where the user lives: covered when an eligible authorization covers one of its countries, not covered when the user states at least one authorization and none of them is an eligible one for its countries, whether the user marked a country as needing a visa ("Remote only in Canada — you need a visa there") or names none of them ("Remote only in Canada — not in your work authorizations"), unknown when the user states no authorization at all ("Add your work authorizations to the profile") or a place is unknown. Remote restriction cannot be compared for a vacancy that is not remote. Onsite and hybrid vacancies are not checked against work authorizations. Only an authorization marked eligible covers a country, and an eligible one in a country of the free-movement zone (the EEA and Switzerland) covers every country of that zone; a visa need in one of them does not spread. Composite labels ("Berlin, Germany", "Remote (US or Canada)") are read with a vocabulary of countries and regions; a part the vocabulary does not know is taken as a city, and a place that names no country or region is unknown.

**Time zone.** The vacancy's bands come from a stated time zone (an offset such as UTC+2 or an abbreviation such as CET, widened by "± N hours"), then from time-zone phrases in the text, then from the countries and regions of its locations.

**Buckets.** Strong is 75 and above, Partial is 55 to 74, Weak is below 55, and Not scored has no number. The Fit filter of the Inbox uses these buckets.

## Requirements

- A vacancy's requirements are the lines of its requirement sections found by code (in a text with no such section, the lines that state a requirement), each with its level, required or nice to have, from its section and marker words. A section starts at a heading: a heading tag, a bold line or a line ending in a colon that holds a known heading phrase, or a short unmarked line (at most 8 words, or 12 with a final colon, and no other final punctuation) that holds a phrase of two words or more ("what we expect", "good fit") when a list or a run of paragraphs follows it; a single word such as "requirements" in an unmarked line counts only when it is the whole line. A heading line is read as one heading, so "Bonus Qualifications" and "Preferred Requirements" are Nice to have, and a short line (at most 6 words) that starts with a known heading word is one heading of its kind; a label inside a sentence ("Our Product Education: we teach robots") is not a heading. A section is read in blocks, cut at the unknown headings inside it. In a block with a bulleted list every line before the list's end is a requirement, and so is every paragraph of a block with no list, as if each were a list item. A line after the last list item, a line after an unknown heading with no list, a later paragraph of a section opened by a label on the same line as its text, and every line after a plain heading-like paragraph count only when they read like a requirement (experience, knowledge, years, a degree and the like) or name a known skill; a paragraph (not a list item) is also dropped when it is about the company or the hiring process, starts with "We", "Our", "This role" or the company's name and states no demand ("we want", "you need", "we expect") and no eligibility condition (visa, sponsorship, residence, work authorization, citizenship and the like). A plain heading-like paragraph is one of two to six words with no final punctuation that does not read like a requirement, not the first of its block, that is followed by another such paragraph, by a paragraph of more than 25 words or by the end of the section; it is not a requirement, and every line after it counts only when it reads like a requirement. A line about pay, benefits, perks, equal opportunity or accommodations, how to apply or the hiring process, a recruitment scam warning or a note about official channels, or a line of hashtags, is never one.
- **Skills.** Every skill the skills vocabulary finds in those lines is one skill of the vacancy: its name, Required or Nice to have (Required when it is in both), the level the vacancy demands and the line it came from. A skill named in several lines is one skill with the highest demand. A line that names alternatives ("C, C++ or Rust", "one of Go or Java") gives one skill naming them, met by the best of them. A slash between two skills ("JavaScript/TypeScript") is such a line too. A phrase that is another name does not count as its skill (Unity Catalog is not Unity, Key Vault not Vault), nor does a skill followed by a company word such as Labs, Inc or Foundation. Names that are also ordinary words count only in their proper form: React, Rails, Airflow, Groovy and Prefect only with a capital letter ("react quickly", "payment rails" and "airflow management" name no skill), and Twilio, EDR, Omni and Sanity only with a capital letter and a technology word near them; "SOC" alone is not SOC 2. A skill glued to the next sentence ("skills.Laravel") is still found.
- **Demanded level.** The skills vocabulary holds a table that reads a level from the line: "1+" years and the words familiarity, exposure and basic mean Beginner; "2+" and "3+" years and the words solid, strong, proficient and hands-on mean Proficient; "4+" years and more and the words deep, expert and extensive mean Expert; a line with no years and no word of the table demands Any, that is, having the skill is enough. Years and words beyond the table follow the nearest rule; of several, the highest counts, and a range of years counts by its lower end. Years apply to every skill of their line.
- **Verdict of a skill.** The user's level comes from Skills in My data (Beginner, Proficient, Expert) or from the skills the user does not have (No). A level at or above the demand is ✓; one level below is borderline, ≈; two below, or No, is ✕; a skill that is in neither list of My data is judged by an assumed level when Experience names it, and is ? otherwise. Experience names a skill when an entry's own skills list names it, or its title or summary names it through the skills vocabulary (aliases and alternatives included). The assumed years are the durations of those entries added up, overlaps counted once and an entry without dates counted as one year; the level is the one the demanded-level table gives for that many years (1 year Beginner, 2 Proficient, 4 Expert, the same table that reads "4+ years" from a line). An entry that ended more than ten years ago counts for at most Beginner, and is named with its dates ("Acme · 2005–2010"); an assumed skill with less than a year is Beginner. A skill in Skills or among the skills the user does not have is never assumed; the vacancy page marks an assumed skill and says which entries it came from ("Acme · 4 years; Beta · 2 years"). A level the user picks for it is written to Skills as for any other skill and replaces the assumption. Any is met by any level.
- **Other requirements.** A line that names no known skill stays a requirement row, decided by code against My data: ✓ or ✕ when the requirement is confirmed by the user as had or as not had, and ? otherwise. A requirement that asks for years of experience is not decided by code; the derived years are shown as evidence. A line that names a skill is not a row: its skills carry it.
- While any skill's verdict is ?, the vacancy page shows "Mark your skills →" at the right of the score column, level with the first row of skills, with a colour wave running through it every few seconds (none when the system asks for reduced motion); a click opens the picker of the first unanswered skill. When every skill is answered, or the vacancy has none, nothing is shown. No count of requirements is shown in a list.
- The requirements score is the average over skills and rows of ✓ = 1, ≈ = 0.5 and ✕ = 0, with required ones counted twice and nice-to-have ones once; undecided ones are left out. Without a decided one it cannot be compared. It enters Fit as the Requirements criterion, and a requirement decided ✕ is a mismatch of it.
- Model matching of requirements is not part of the first version. A later checker can be connected through the same interface, and only after it decides hand-labelled examples better than code.

## Facts of a vacancy

Facts are found by code, without a model, for every vacancy visible in a profile, as the background work "Analyzing vacancy" (see `application.md`): money, work mode, engagement, place, time zone, hours, required languages, the requirement lines, and eight key facts: the years of experience asked, the seniority, the company stage, the deadline for applications, the length of a contract, a citizenship or security-clearance requirement, relocation support and visa sponsorship. Structured fields the source states, the location, the tags and labelled lines ("Salary: …", "Location: …", "Time zone: …") are read first, then vocabularies over the whole text; each fact keeps the excerpt it was found in. When the address is an aggregator's, a value stated in the vacancy text replaces a field's value of the same kind; when it is the employer's own page, a field or labelled line replaces a vocabulary's value. Fit and the tiles read these facts and nothing else.

One fact is read by a model: the **main domain** of the company, one of infrastructure, DeFi, exchange, wallet and custody, payments, fintech, AI, security, data, gaming, gambling, prediction markets, adtech, e-commerce, health, education, government and defence, media, NFT, SaaS, hardware and other. Fit compares it with the profile's domain lists like any other list criterion (see Lists). The model reads the start of the company's newest vacancy (up to 4,000 characters) and judges what the company's business is, not what the role does, and a domain is kept only with a sentence that the text contains; when the text never says what the company does, there is no domain. A company is read once, and all its vacancies share the answer until the text or the reading changes. With "Precise reading" on (see `settings-and-data.md`) every new vacancy that is visible, undecided and without a broken must is read, newest first; with it off, a vacancy is read when the user opens it. A vacancy read only by the model is still not analysed until the code has analysed it.

A fact says only what the vacancy says; when the vacancy does not say it, the fact is absent and the tile shows "?", never a guess with ✓.

- **Years, seniority, company stage.** Years is the most years the requirement lines ask for experience in general or in the role's field ("5+ years of backend experience" is 5, "five years" and "5-year" count too; a range such as "3-5 years" counts its lower end), never the years of a company's history or product, and not a line marked nice to have. When no requirement line asks years, a sentence anywhere outside the company, benefits, equal-opportunity and how-to-apply parts that asks "N years of/in/with … experience" is read; "founded 12 years ago" and "for over 10 years we have …" are never read. Seniority (Intern, Junior, Mid, Senior, Staff, Principal, Lead, Head, Director, VP, C-level) comes from the title first, then from a plain statement in the text ("this is a senior-level role"); a look-alike such as "Lead Generation" or "Chief of Staff" gives none, and a title that names several levels gives the highest. Company stage comes only from the opening text and the company sections, never from a description of investors or customers ("our customers are VC-backed startups"): Public, Series A to E (the latest named), Seed, Bootstrapped, Profitable, Growth (only an explicit "growth-stage" or "scale-up"; revenue growth gives none), a raise without a series ("Raised $120M"), Early stage ("early-stage", "pre-seed") and VC-backed ("backed by … investors", "venture-backed"), the first of these in that order that the text states. A raise states its amount when the same sentence does, and its valuation after it ("Series D, $1.37B, valued at $7.87B"); an amount that is revenue, ARR or users is not a raise. The phrases are in the vocabulary shipped with the application.
- **Engagement.** Contract comes only from a statement of the engagement ("contract role", "contractor basis", "B2B contract", "1099", "fixed-term", "freelance", "as a contractor"), not from "smart contract", "contract management", "contractors" in equal-opportunity text, a negated statement ("this is not a contract role", "do not apply to fixed term roles") "temporary work visa" and "permanent residency" (a word about work authorization), or the company being a government or federal contractor; a line of equal-opportunity or self-identification boilerplate gives none. A label such as Type, Job type or Employment type followed by Contract, Freelance or B2B (also as a table cell under such a header), "N-month contract" and "Contract, N+ months" give contract. An internship also comes from an intern in the title, "hiring interns" and "Interns are paid", not from "mentor interns". An internship is its own value, Internship, never contract. "Full-time" and "part-time" are hours and never make Employment; Employment comes from "permanent", "W-2", "full-time employee", "employment contract", "employee position", or a site field that says permanent or employee. Text under requirements, nice-to-have and how-to-apply gives no engagement.
- **Place.** A restriction anywhere in the text, including benefits, eligibility notes and application questions, gives the place ("Remote, US-based", "Remote - Poland", "anywhere in Canada", "must reside in Spain", "authorized to work in X", "must be a US citizen") and replaces a site field's place, for an employer's vacancy too ("West Coast (U.S.) based" is the United States). "Anywhere" or "Remote" as the place in an aggregator's field, with nothing in the text, is no place, but "Remote" and TELECOMMUTE in a location field are the work mode Remote, and the countries listed with them stay. A restriction in the title ("Remote - LATAM") beats a softer phrase in the text, and a place that describes customers, users or developers served is no restriction. "Work from anywhere" and "worldwide remote" are explicit worldwide statements. A soft request to overlap some working hours with a team's time zone is not a time-zone restriction. A worldwide statement in the text gives Worldwide only when no site field names a place; a country or city in a site field stays, while a restriction in the text still replaces it. Worldwide comes only from an explicit statement ("work from anywhere in the world", "hiring worldwide", "global and remote-based") and never beside a country, region or time-zone restriction, whether it comes from the text or from a site field. A bare "Location" line takes the first sentence of the next line as its value when that sentence is mostly a list of places ("Denver or Austin. Hybrid office setting."), and a label glued to the end of the previous word ("…(India)Location: Bengaluru") is read even after another label on the line, and the places stop where the sentence that follows begins. A value that names no place ("LOCATION", "na", a two-letter word that is not a country code) is dropped; a country code from a site field shows as the country name ("PL" as Poland).
- **Hours and languages.** "Full-time" or "part-time" is no Hours when it describes other people or a condition and not this job: before "experience" ("3+ years of full-time experience"), before employees, staff or roles ("benefits for all full-time employees"), after "authorized to work" ("authorized to work in the US full-time") and around a return ("return part-time after parental leave"); "a full-time position" and "Full time/40 hours" stay Hours. A language is read by its English, German or Spanish name and level words ("Stilsichere Deutschkenntnisse", "Fliessende Sprachkenntnisse in Deutsch und Englisch", "Español nativo e Inglés avanzado") as well as by its English ones.
- **Money.** Pay is only the role's pay: amounts tied to bonuses, stipends, budgets, allowances, learning, equipment, referrals, sign-on or per-task payments, a range written backwards, and an amount below a plausible floor for its period (and any amount with no period below 10,000) are not money. "$175K–$225K + equity", "£115k + equity", "272 000 zł – 368 000 zł" and a typo such as "$180,000-$200,00" are read.
- **Work mode.** "In-person N days a week", "Willing to work from <city>", "Location: In person" and "in-person" for the role give Onsite; a share of the time in the office ("at least 40%") and days in the office with remote days give Hybrid; "<city> or Remote" gives Remote and Onsite. In-person interviews, events and meetings give no mode, "in-office" in a perk such as a stipend gives none, a business "hybrid model" or "hybrid financing" in a sentence with no word about the workplace gives none, and "hybrid" with freedom to work remotely stays Hybrid. "HYBRID, 2+ days in office" is Hybrid only. A header segment such as "NYC or REMOTE (US, Europe)" gives Remote and Onsite. "This role is based in our <city> HQ" gives Onsite, "50% telecommuting" gives Hybrid, and a location field such as "In person, San Francisco Bay Area" gives Onsite and the place.

## In the lists

- Every row in all four lists shows Fit as a filled mark with its bucket. The tooltip reads "Fit 82: Strong".
- Empty states of Fit: "Not compared yet", "Full job description is unavailable", "Your profile states no goals to compare", "Nothing the vacancy states can be compared with your goals".
- A row whose vacancy is not analysed yet says so in place of a count: "Analysing requirements…", "Not analysed yet" or "Full job description is unavailable"; an analysed vacancy says nothing.
- A result is out of date when the profile's goals, the work authorizations, the languages, the weights, the rates or the scoring version changed, or the vacancy's text changed. It is dimmed, still showing its old number, until the new one is ready, and the Fit filter counts it as Not scored. A change of the vacancy's title, location, tags or stated fields without a change of its text is not dimmed; its result is recomputed after the collection run that brought the change.
- **Best fit** sorts by Fit, highest first, then by id; dimmed results and vacancies without a number come after the current ones, by id; vacancies with a broken must come last, by their number. It is the default sort of Inbox and Shortlist.
- **Newest** sorts by publication date, newest first; vacancies with no publication date come last, by when they were first seen, then by id.
- The chosen sort is remembered in the browser (see `inbox.md`).

## On the vacancy page

From top to bottom:

1. **Fit band.** "Fit" above one large number coloured by its bucket (Strong, Partial, Weak; no colour for Not scored), the reason in place of the number when the vacancy is not scored, and the "Refresh source" button (see `collection.md`); the reasons and "scored on" are not shown there, since the tiles and Skills say the same. When any tile's value is in a Never list, a "Never" line above the band names each one ("Work mode: Onsite is in your Never list"). On the right, the key facts of the vacancy, each with its icon and name, a verdict mark ✓ (green), ✕ (red), – (neutral) or ? (grey), the vacancy's value or "Not stated", and a line comparing the value with the user's goal. Four are cards, in the order Money, Location, Work mode, Contract; the rest are rows of the Key facts table below them, in the order Time zone, Languages, Hours, Work authorization, with mismatches first, then unknown, then matches, followed by Experience, Seniority and Company stage, then Domain, which is shown when the vacancy states it or the profile has a domain goal, and then, each only when the vacancy states it, Apply by, Contract length, Citizenship or clearance, Relocation and Visa. These five show – and do not change Fit. Domain shows the vacancy's main domain by its label ("Exchange"), with the line "Read by the model" and the mark –; with a domain goal it is a criterion row: ✓ or ✕ and the colour of its list, the line naming the list ("Exchange — preferred · read by the model"), "Not stated" and ? with the line "Domain not stated" while the company is not read, and – with "Gaming — not in your lists · read by the model" for a domain in no list. Apply by shows the date and how many days are left ("12 days left", "Closes today") with – and is ✕ once the date has passed ("Closed 3 days ago"); a date without a year is read as the one nearest to the day the vacancy was first seen. Contract length shows the length with its unit ("6 months", "16 weeks", "4–8 months"), Citizenship or clearance the requirement as the text names it ("US citizen", "Active Secret clearance", "U.S. person (ITAR)", "Public trust"), Relocation "Offered" or "Not offered" and Visa "Sponsorship offered" or "No sponsorship". A card also shows the conversion when there is one and the line.

A tile never disagrees with the number; its line is the reason of its criterion ("Onsite — in your Never list"). A criterion is compared when the profile has a goal for it: a minimum compensation, a locations, work mode, engagement, working time or domain list, or time zones. Languages and Work authorization have no goal of their own and count when the vacancy requires a language, or is remote. In the Key facts table every criterion of Fit is shown, a row whose value is not stated with "Not stated" and ?, so a band full of ? says at a glance that the number rests on little. A fact that is no criterion is shown only when the vacancy states it. Time zone shows the stated time zone, or else the bands of the vacancy's countries; Languages the required languages; Hours full-time or part-time with the weekly hours; Work authorization the place a remote vacancy is restricted to, or "No restriction", and "Not checked" with – for a vacancy that is not remote. Money shows the vacancy's own sum compactly: the currency symbol where one exists ($, €, £) and otherwise the code ("CHF 120k"), then the amount or the range, written in thousands or millions with one decimal when it is not whole ("$190–225k", "€7.5k", "€900–1.2M") and in full below a thousand, then the period the vacancy states ("a year"). Its conversion, "≈ €207,000", stands beside that sum and is absent when no rate is known. A tile also carries the list its value fell in (`bucket`: preferred, possible, unwanted or never; absent when the value is in no list or the criterion has none), so the client can colour it. The Contract tile shows "Not stated" when the vacancy states no engagement and no longer includes hours. The Location tile shows its places in the list row's short form (see `inbox.md`), with the full text on hover when shortened.

Experience shows the years asked as "5+ years" with the derived total years of My data as its line ("You have 9 years"): ✓ when the user's years reach the years asked, ✕ when they fall short by more than two years, and ? otherwise, also when My data has no experience. Seniority and Company stage show the value ("Senior", "Series B, $40M") with –.

   **Skills** stand under the Key facts table, in two columns headed "Skills required" and "Skills nice to have", with no count. Each skill is its own chip, ordered within its column as the verdicts say: what the user lacks (✕) first, then what is unknown (?), then what the user has (≈, then ✓). The alternatives of one requirement ("C++ or Rust") are separate chips side by side. A chip names the skill and shows the user's own level when My data has one, not the level the vacancy demands, which the picker shows ("They ask: Proficient"). Its colour is ✕ when the user does not have that skill, unknown (dashed) when My data says nothing of it, and otherwise the verdict of the requirement.
   - A click on a chip opens its picker: the line the skill came from, "They ask: Proficient" when the vacancy demands a level, the user's level from My data, the Shortlist check's answer when there is one, and the levels Expert, Proficient, Beginner and No. For a chip of alternatives the picker says "Any one of C++ or Rust is enough". Choosing a level writes My data for the skill of that chip: the skill at that level in Skills, taking it out of the skills the user does not have; No puts it among the skills the user does not have and takes it out of Skills. A skill the user holds under another name of the vocabulary changes that entry and is not added twice. Clearing an answer is done in the profile.

2. **Other requirements.** A full-width section below the band, folded when the page opens; its header says "Other requirements" and how many there are ("Other requirements (2)"), and it is left out when there are none. Unfolded, it lists the requirement rows, the lines that name no known skill, each with its verdict, the evidence from My data ("8 years", for a line that asks for years), and, while the line is undecided, two buttons, **I have it** and **I don't**, which add the line to Confirmed by you as "You have: …" or "You do not have: …"; after that the row shows ✓ or ✕ and "Confirmed by you".
   - After an answer this vacancy is recomputed at once, on the page and in its Inbox row; other vacancies follow in the background.

3. **Listing text**, described in `inbox.md`.
4. **Shortlist check**, in Shortlist and Archive when a check exists (see below).

## Shortlist check

- When a vacancy enters Shortlist, one model call compares the user's data with the vacancy's requirements. When that call decides no requirement at all, it is made once more, and the second answer is the answer. The model receives the vacancy's facts and text, the accepted data of My data and the profile including other requirements, and the text of documents marked "Use as evidence"; never contacts, legal name or application form answers (see `profiles.md`).
- Its answer is shown next to the requirements that code left as ?, each with a short reason and the excerpt it relies on; a skill that is ? goes to it as the line it came from, and its answer is shown beside that skill. It changes neither Fit nor the counter.
- When any of its inputs changes, the check is marked "Based on your data of <date>" and runs again the next time the vacancy page is opened. A change of profile or My data therefore makes no model call by itself.
- States: "Checking…", "Waiting for the model: N ahead", and "Check failed: <reason>" with a "Retry" button shown only after a failure or an interruption. There is no Assess or Reassess button.
- The check uses the model chosen for it in Settings; by default it must be a local model or one the user chose explicitly.

## When things are recomputed

| Change | What is recomputed | Model calls |
|---|---|---|
| New or changed vacancy text | its facts and requirements are found; its Fit for every profile, after that | none |
| A vacancy becomes visible in a profile without having been analysed | its facts and requirements are found | none |
| A change of goals or My data that enters Fit | Fit of every vacancy for the affected profiles, in the background; Shortlist checks marked outdated | none |
| A level on a skill, or + or − on a requirement row | this vacancy at once, the rest in the background | none |
| A change of weights or vocabularies | everything | none |
| A new version of the application that reads texts differently | the facts of every vacancy are found again, in the background | none |
| A vacancy enters Shortlist | the Shortlist check | 1, or 2 when the first decides nothing |
| A shortlisted vacancy with an outdated check is opened | the Shortlist check | 1, or 2 when the first decides nothing |

Fit is recomputed in the background by code, with no model call: at start, after a collection run, and after My data or a profile is saved or a profile is created. Only results that are missing or out of date are recomputed, and the work repeats until none is left, so a change made while it runs is applied too.

Vacancy texts are analysed in the background, one at a time, in the order the vacancies arrived, and only for vacancies visible in at least one profile; Fit is recomputed for them after their analysis, not before. Analysis is code and makes no model call.
