# Changelog

Notable changes of Sifty, newest first. Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`.

## Unreleased

- Applicant tracking boards are one module each, with the hosts they own and the shape of a vacancy address on them; a vacancy on a European Greenhouse or European Lever board is now recognised as a board vacancy, so the same vacancy from two sources is matched as one and a company exclusion matches its apply address.
- Documents: a CV or a note arrives as a file (pdf, md or txt, at most 2 MiB) or as pasted text, keeps its extracted text and its original, is listed newest first, can be marked "Use as evidence" and can be deleted with its file; the text of a pdf is read by the `pdftotext` program of poppler when it is installed, and a pdf stored before that is read again by `POST /api/documents/{id}/refresh`; when the program is missing the document says how to install it, and nothing is analysed by a model.
- Web client 0.11.1: the interface description is in step with the server; the client itself is unchanged.
- The budget of the backend code is 18,500 lines instead of 16,000: the capabilities already delivered are 2,294 lines above the estimates they were given, and the estimates of the capabilities still to come were left as they are.
- Web client 0.11.0: Settings has a Models section with the kind of model work and its provider, the providers with their cost and state, and the background work switches, the daily limit and the day's count; a settings file restored from a backup is named under the header.
- Settings › Models has a server side: one address lists the providers with their cost and state, the provider chosen for each kind of model work, the paid-bulk permission, the pause switch, the daily limit of background calls and how many were made today, and changes any of them; the separate pause address is gone.
- Web client 0.10.0: a tile of the Fits you band shows its converted amount on its own line under the value.
- The money of a vacancy is written compactly, "$190–225k a year", and the approximate euro amount, rounded to the nearest thousand, stands on its own beside it instead of inside the same line.
- Web client 0.9.0: the vacancy page shows What they offer beside What they ask, with a tile per benefit the listing names and an "Also offered" list.
- What they offer (server): the vacancy view carries a "Base salary" tile with the band's money value, tiles for equity, health, time off, retirement, learning, equipment, bonus and flexibility read from the listing's own compensation and benefits sections, and the rest of those sections as "Also offered"; when no such section is recognised, or one is found with nothing in it, the block says so.
- Web client 0.8.0: a requirement that names several known skills offers one + per skill and one + for the requirement as a whole; answering for one skill records that skill alone and leaves the other choices in place.
- Web client 0.7.0: the vacancy page lists what the employer asks, grouped Required and Nice to have, with the verdict, alternatives and evidence of each requirement, and + and − on the ones code could not decide.
- What they ask (server): the vacancy view lists requirements in Required and Nice to have with verdict, alternatives and evidence (matched skills, Confirmed by you, or derived years when the requirement states years); `POST /api/me/skills` adds a requirement's skills or statement to My data with + and −, decides it, and recomputes the vacancy at once.
- Web client 0.6.0: the requirements counter beside Fits you in list rows, with the state in the tooltip, and under the bucket in the vacancy band.
- Requirements counter: each requirement of a vacancy is decided by code against your skills, the skills you do not have and Confirmed by you, using an embedded skills vocabulary; Inbox rows and the vacancy band show "✓ 4 · ✕ 1 · ? 6" or the state (Analysing requirements…, Not analysed yet, No requirements stated, Not checked yet). The Requirements criterion of Fits you has weight 0 until it is set.
- Vacancy texts visible in a profile are read by the model in the background, one at a time, in arrival order; a busy, paused or limited model postpones the queue without using up attempts; the call journal names the vacancy.
- A model reader of vacancy texts: requirements with kind, level and alternatives, and the conditions code did not find, each tied to an exact excerpt of the text; the Ollama and OpenAI-compatible adapters send the answer schema, and reading runs without the model's thinking.
- The previous version's model readings are imported and Fits you uses their conditions where code found none; readings are kept per vacancy with the version and reader they belong to.
- Command-line model providers work: the Claude command line and opencode, run with a reduced environment and no tools.
- Web client 0.5.0: Settings no longer has an industries editor.
- Industries come from what sources say: Getro company labels through an embedded table, and the industry of boards devoted to one (web3.career, Cryptocurrency Jobs, Remote3); keyword matching, the industries editor and its background reassignment are removed; a vacancy keeps every industry any source gave it.
- Board platforms state more: a16z and Paradigm records give salary, remote work and locations, and Paradigm lists every vacancy on its board, including those on other sites; Getro gives work mode, salary and locations; Ashby gives workplace type, salary with its period and every location; Lever keeps every location and the salary period.
- Collection reads what sites state: We Work Remotely and Remotive feed fields (employment type, region or country, expiry), JSON-LD salary objects, employment type arrays and applicant location requirements, Hacker News first-line tokens (work mode, employment type, salary); more spellings of employment type, work mode, salary period and dates are understood; Fits you is recomputed.
- Known sites are described in an embedded `sites.yaml` that `<data>/vocab/sites.yaml` can extend or override by host; a broken settings or site file no longer stops the start: it is renamed, the newest backup (or the defaults) is restored, and the recovery is listed in the status; unknown settings keys are ignored and reported.
- Model providers (Ollama, OpenAI-compatible endpoints, command-line tools) with a gate for background work: one call per provider at a time, a pause switch, a daily limit of background calls and a journal of calls; providers and their checks in the status; providers imported from the previous version.
- Sources are read by one module per kind of site with one table of known sites; no visible change.
- The import leaves out vacancies without full text that no profile acted on, without a removal mark, so collection can gather them again.
- Web client 0.4.1: no comments in its sources; interface synced.
- Fits you treats a vacancy that states no engagement as employment; the Contract tile shows "Employment (assumed)".
- No comments in code, migrations, embedded YAML, the Makefile or scripts; `make uncomment` removes them and `make check` refuses them.
- The built-in web client is sifty-web 0.4.0: Fits you on every row, the Best fit / Newest switch, the Fits you filter group and the vacancy band with four tiles.
- Every list row carries Fits you (number, bucket and tooltip, dimmed while out of date, or the reason there is none); Inbox and Shortlist sort by Best fit or Newest; the Fit filter (Strong, Partial, Weak, Not scored) works in all lists; the vacancy page carries the Fits you band with four tiles. Fits you is stored per profile and vacancy and recomputed in the background after collection, after a change of My data or a profile, and at start.
- Fits you is computed by code: the criteria with their weights, the Location rules, the countries-and-regions resolver, buckets and the four tiles; the settings file takes `weights` and `rates`. `sifty import-v1` carries the previous weights and rates, compares Fits you of every vacancy with the previous version in its report and writes `fit-comparison.tsv`; it imports feed texts as full text and keeps the line structure of plain texts.
- A source added while the server runs is named on the vacancy page at once. Start no longer rebuilds the full-text index or reassigns industries; a vacancy seen again unchanged keeps its full-text row; the status reads only each source's last three runs. The Markdown dependency is gone.
- A profile's empty target industries are inherited from the first profile, the one with the lowest id, also after the profile created first was deleted.
- web3.career vacancies carry their company, from the listing row and from their own page; a vacancy page with several JSON-LD items without addresses is matched by a unique title, and a page title "Role at Company" fills an empty company. `sifty import-v1` recovers a missing company from the address of We Work Remotely, Getro and web3.career vacancies.
- The built-in web client is sifty-web 0.3.1: the filter panel no longer offers Hide expired.
- Vacancies past their expiry date are left out of Inbox, Shortlist and Dismissed, their counts and search; Applied still shows them with "Listing expired". The `hideExpired` list parameter is gone.
- The built-in web client is sifty-web 0.3.0: industries as badges in rows and on the vacancy page, Settings → Industries, and the Unread and Filters controls with Collected, Role, Industries and Sources.
- Once a day, and at start, the server removes every vacancy whose expiry date passed more than a day ago and that has no application from any profile; collection keeps it away afterwards, as after "Delete role…". Vacancies with an application stay and show "Listing expired".
- Lists filter by first-seen date (7 or 30 days), hide expired vacancies, keep only remote or leadership roles, keep only chosen sources, and keep only unread vacancies; each response counts the vacancies per industry and per source with the other filters applied, and gives the stage total before the query and filters.
- The built-in web client is sifty-web 0.2.0: the profile page with the switcher, every section in read and edit mode with inheritance, the photo, Questions & Answers and per-profile exclusions.
- The interface description gives a profile's inherited fields as an object of field name to source profile; before, it called it a string.
- Every vacancy carries its industries, matched by the keywords of an editable vocabulary of industries (whole words and phrases in the title, tags and text); `GET/PUT /api/industries` reads and saves the vocabulary, and saving reassigns every vacancy in the background. Lists show each vacancy's industries, filter by industry and count each one. A profile's target industries, own or inherited, keep out of its Inbox the vacancies whose industries are all elsewhere; names not in the vocabulary are ignored. `sifty import-v1` assigns industries to every vacancy and lists, without importing, the previous free-text target industries.
- `sifty import-v1` brings My data, every field of each profile, the active profile, the photo and Questions & Answers; previous free-text fields without a structured value are kept in Other requirements, and the report lists every value folded or dropped. An empty section of a profile is stored as empty, so it is taken from the first profile.
- The server keeps My data and every profile with all their fields, checks them as the profile page does, and fills an empty goal of a profile from the first profile. Profiles can be added, renamed, deleted (not the first) and made active; a list without a profile shows the active one. Word exclusions can belong to one profile. The photo, Questions & Answers and the lists the editors offer (countries, regions, time-zone bands, option labels) are served too. A new database names its first profile "Profile 1".
- Each profile's Inbox shows only the vacancies that fit its search: target roles in the title and keywords in the title, tags or text (both when both are set), every "All of these" term, matched as whole words and phrases; exclusions of one profile hide vacancies in that profile only. `sifty import-v1` brings each profile's name, target roles, keywords, "All of these" terms and except words. In search, punctuation in the text counts as a space.
- A vacancy text keeps every item of a list nested inside another list; before, such items were dropped or ran together.
- The web client moved to its own repository, sifty-works/sifty-web; this repository is now sifty-works/sifty-server. A build embeds a pinned web client release, checked by its checksum, and a release refuses a web client built against another interface description. `GET /api/version` gives the build version and the checksum of the interface description.
- The web client takes its types from the generated interface description and follows the server's actions: the target stage of each decision and the answers of the outcome dialog come from the server.
- The interface between the server and its clients is described in one generated file, `api/openapi.yaml` (OpenAPI 3.1), checked on every build; the vacancy page gives its sources as names with addresses, each action its target stage, dialog and outcome answers, and starting a collection answers with the status instead of a redirect.
- The application looks as before: the black header with the stage tabs, the logo and Settings; the graphite list beside the light vacancy page with its white cards; the primary actions with their key caps, the Apply control with Confirm and Delete below it; the outcome and Delete dialogs; Settings grouped as before, with Source sites shown as cards with switches. Fonts ship with the application.
- Select (Y), Dismiss (N), Move to Inbox, Undo and Restore on the vacancy page; decided Inbox roles stay on screen until reload; Apply (A) opens the employer's page and asks whether the application was submitted; Delete asks before removing a role for good.
- Inbox, Shortlist and Archive (Applied and Dismissed) tabs with their counts; the list sits beside the vacancy page, whose width can be dragged; J and K move through the list, unread roles are bold until opened, and the page shows where the role was found, its status, its actions and the listing text.
- The server answers the vacancy page: where the role was found, the original listing, its status, the actions of its stage with the apply address, and the listing text with its state; an expired listing is marked in the list too.
- `sifty import-v1` also brings the profiles that are not archived, their decisions (saved becomes Shortlist, rejected Dismissed with its reason), the confirmed applications and the read marks, and reports any difference between a vacancy's old status and its first profile's decision.
- The server keeps decisions per profile: Inbox, Shortlist, Applied and Dismissed lists with their counts, moves between them with Undo and Restore, a confirmed application recorded with its date, read marks, and permanent Delete outside Inbox that collection never brings back; merged vacancies keep the strongest decision.
- Cryptocurrency Jobs, Framework Ventures, a16z Frontier and the monthly Hacker News "Who is hiring?" thread are read too; a feed item titled "Role at Company" gives its title and company.
- Collection also skips the page of a vacancy on the Greenhouse board of a blocked company, as the lists already hid it.
- A source whose site does not answer shows "Site not answering"; "Timed out" is kept for a request that ran out of time.
- Settings → Exclusions: words and companies that hide vacancies everywhere, with "Would hide N roles" while typing; "Block company" on an Inbox row. Collection skips the page of an excluded vacancy, and removing an exclusion shows its vacancies at once. The import brings the old stop words and blocked companies.
- A new vacancy found without a description gets its text, company and location from its own page; a page that could not be read is tried again after 7 days, and a site that blocks reading marks the vacancy "Blocked by site".
- Any other site is read too: job postings described on its page, its feed, a Getro board, or links that look like vacancies; We Work Remotely and Remote3 are read through their feeds and web3.career through its vacancy links.
- A site that blocked Sifty is left alone for 24 hours even across a restart.
- Each source on the Sources page shows its last success with vacancies seen and new, or the error of its last run in words, or "No vacancies in 30 days"; sources failing three runs in a row are marked, and the header shows "N sources need attention".
- A role that moves from one applicant tracking system to another (same company and title) stays one vacancy; a board's name taken from its address no longer replaces a known company name.
- Vacancy texts no longer run words together where the source wrapped a space in bold or italics, and keep no empty bold or italic marks.
- Settings → Sources: add, switch off and remove sources, set automatic collection and its interval, and start a collection with "Collect jobs". Collection reads Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby boards and RSS or Atom feeds, shows its progress in the header, and new vacancies appear in the Inbox without reloading the page.
- The Inbox lists the stored vacancies, newest first, with company, title, location and publication date, and searches title, company, tags and text on the server (`/` focuses the search field).
- `sifty import-v1` imports the vacancies, the places they were seen, merged ids, removed addresses, the sources that still had vacancies and the time of the last collection from the previous version.
- The settings file holds the collection switch, the interval (15 minutes to 7 days) and the sources, and keeps its comments when the application changes it.
- `sifty serve` starts on an empty data folder without any setup and refuses a second copy on the same folder; opening it shows an empty Inbox with a hint to add sources; `sifty version` and `sifty config defaults`.
